# Slalom Brand Guidelines — Skill Reference

> Distilled from the Slalom Brand Guide (February 2025) and Solution Naming Guidance (May 2025). Use this as a reference when creating any Slalom-branded content, copy, presentations, or assets.

## 1. Brand Foundation

### Purpose
We help people and organizations dream bigger, move faster, and build better tomorrows for all.

### Vision
A world in which each person has the opportunity to love their life and work.

### Essence: "Fiercely Human"
- Always keep "fiercely" and "human" together — never separate them.
- Use as a descriptor: "fiercely human consulting," "our fiercely human approach."
- It is NOT a tagline. It is the essence of the brand.
- Never call things "fierce" on their own.
- Never imply Slalom makes people "more human."

### Who We Are (Boilerplate)
Slalom is a fiercely human business and technology consulting company that teams with leaders who expect more. So we bring more.

1. Do what is right, always.
2. Drive connection and teamwork.
3. Inspire passion and adventure.
4. Take ownership. Get it done.
5. Celebrate authenticity.
6. Focus on outcomes.
7. Fuel growth and innovation.
8. Stay humble and curious.
9. Build and shape a better future.
10. Smile.

### Key Differentiators
- **Customer Love** — We measure customer love in 10 dimensions.
- **Local Soul, Global Scale** — Invested in local communities with global strength.
- **Momentum That Outlasts Us** — We empower teams to keep going after we leave.
- **Head and Heart** — We lead with empathy and kindness.
- **Deep Partnerships** — Trusted partner relationships with major tech companies.

## 2. Creative Lens — "Is It Fiercely Human?"

Use this filter for ALL content — written and visual:

## 3. Voice & Writing Style

### Tone
Clear, confident, and conversational. Helpful and optimistic.

### Writing Tips
- **Appeal to emotions and needs** — Put yourself in the reader's shoes.
- **Address the reader directly** — Use "you" instead of "the client."
- **Customer is always the hero** — Frame benefits from their perspective, not Slalom's.
- **Be conversational** — Write as if explaining to a friend.
- **Write crisply** — Cut to the chase. Simple sentences work.
- **Be clear** — Don't assume the audience will read between the lines.
- **Avoid jargon** — If a friend wouldn't understand it, rephrase.
- **Use active voice** where possible.
- **Use metrics** to back up arguments. Cite sources.

### Terminology
- **Slalomers** — Informal/colloquial for employees. Use "Slalom team members" for formal/external.
- **Customers** — Preferred over "clients." As in "our customers' customers."

### Style Basics (Chicago Manual of Style with exceptions)
- **Capitalization**: Sentence case for headlines and subheads. Minimize title capitalization.
- **Abbreviations**: Spell out on first reference, then abbreviate in parentheses.
- **Job titles**: Lowercase in body copy (e.g., senior consultant, managing director). Capitalize in formal settings.
- **Industries**: Lowercase in body copy (e.g., healthcare, financial services). Capitalize in lists.
- **Em dash (—)**: Closed, no spaces. "Three writers—Ruth, Randi, and David—agreed."
- **Numbers**: Spell out zero to nine; digits for 10+. Flexible in headlines.
- **Percent**: Use numerals + "%" (e.g., 44%). Spell out at sentence start.
- **Pronouns**: Use singular "they." Respect individuals' pronouns.
- **Spaces**: One space between sentences and after colons.
- **Inclusive language**: Avoid biased language, idioms with problematic history.

| Color | HEX | RGB |
|---|---|---|

| **Light Gray** (10% black) | `#E6E6E6` | 230/230/230 |

### Color Rules
- A Slalom Blue (regular or dark) **MUST be present in every composition**.
- Pair blue with only **ONE secondary color** per composition to avoid "rainbowy" effect.
- Do not use all colors at once (exception: official color bar or infographic charts).
- Each secondary color has 3 dark tones and 3 light tints available for depth.

### Color Bar
- Colors appear in fixed order: Slalom Blue → Cyan → Coral Red → Purple → Chartreuse.
- One color bar per layout max.
- The Slalom Blue framing line extends the full height or width of the layout.

## 5. Typography

### Brand Fonts (All materials EXCEPT PowerPoint)
- **Slalom Sans** — Primary font. Weights: Thin, Light, Regular, Bold, Black (+ italics).
- **Lora** — Supporting serif font. Optional. Weights: Regular, Medium, Semibold, Bold (+ italics).

### PowerPoint ONLY Font
- **Avenir Next LT Pro** — Used exclusively in PowerPoint decks for compatibility. Also acceptable for MS Outlook emails, Power BI dashboards, and editable Word docs shared with clients.
- **Never use Avenir Next LT Pro** for social media, print, video, digital, or any non-PowerPoint format.

### Type Styling
| Element | Font | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Headlines (default) | Slalom Sans Bold | Sentence case |
| Headlines (emphasis) | Slalom Sans Black | Sentence case |
| Headlines (soft) | Lora Regular | Sentence case |
| Subheads (eyebrow) | SLALOM SANS BOLD | All caps, tracking 75 |
| Subheads (default) | Slalom Sans Bold | Sentence case |
| Body (default) | Slalom Sans Light | Leading 1.1, tracking 0 |
| Body (small/legibility) | Slalom Sans Regular | Leading 1.1, tracking 0 |

### Type Rules
- Max 3 type sizes per composition.
- Sentence case is standard. All-caps only for short headings/eyebrows.
- Always flush left, ragged right. Never justified or centered.
- Use round bullets (bold weight).

## 6. Logo

### Usage
- Logo is **lowercase wordmark only**. No secondary logos exist.
- Approved colors: **Slalom Blue, Slalom Dark Blue, White, or Black** only.
- Never recolor, rotate, skew, add effects, or alter the logo in any way.
- Minimum size: 0.125 inches / 9 pixels high.
- Clear space: At least the width of the "o" in the logo on all sides.

### Preferred Lockups
1. **Primary**: Slalom Blue or Dark Blue logo on white background.
2. **Alternate backgrounds**: White logo on Slalom Blue, Light Gray, or Dark Blue.
3. **Secondary color backgrounds**: Slalom Blue or Dark Blue logo (white reversed on Coral Red or dark tones).
4. **Black/White**: Reserved for B&W-only layouts.

### Sub-Branding
- Slalom logo may be locked up with a descriptor (market, product, service, team) using **Slalom Sans Light** at -10 tracking.
- **Never create new logos** for any product, service, or program. No exceptions.
- Ask: Does it uplevel the brand? Could it increase confusion? Is the name clear and succinct?

## 7. Solution Naming Convention

### Formula
```
Slalom [Partner/Tech]* [Descriptor] [What It Is] for [Function/Industry]*
```
*Items marked with * are optional based on context.*

### Core Principle: "Call it what it is."
- Use clear, simple, descriptive language — the name should explain itself.
- Lead with "Slalom" externally to drive brand connection.
- Convey the purpose or benefit for the target audience.
- Keep it memorable and to the point.

### Components

| Component | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Slalom** | Brand consistency. Always lead externally. | Slalom |
| **Partner/Tech** | Specifies technology or partner. Use their product name if built for a specific partner. | AWS, Salesforce, Microsoft, Snowflake, Adobe |
| **Descriptor** | 1–2 words describing the service/focus, value, or what it enables. | AI, Cloud, Data Governance, Workforce, Security, Pricing |
| **What It Is** | Type classification so audience knows what to expect. | Accelerator, Offering, Assessment, Workshop, Agent, Methodology, Transformation |
| **Function/Industry** | Optional. Only if there's a specific functional or industry focus. Must use Slalom-approved industry names. | Financial Services, Healthcare, HR, Marketing, IT |

### "What It Is" Definitions
- **Accelerator** — Reusable code/components to speed project execution.
- **Offering** — Services with defined deliverables.
- **Assessment** — Structured evaluation of current state.
- **Workshop** — Interactive collaborative session.
- **Agent** — AI-powered autonomous program for tasks/decisions.
- **Methodology** — Structured approach guiding planning and delivery.
- **Transformation** — Comprehensive initiative redefining a business area.

### Do NOT use as "What It Is": Software, Platform, Product (Slalom is not structured to sell these).

### Good Name Examples
- Slalom AI-Accelerated Engineering
- Slalom Amazon Buy with Prime Accelerator
- Slalom Emission Measurement Accelerator for Financial Services
- Slalom Salesforce Health Cloud Migration Assessment for Med Tech
- Slalom Adaptive Workforce Offering

### Bad Name Patterns
| Bad | Better | Why |
|---|---|---|
| EnhanceIQ | Slalom AI-Powered Workforce Accelerator | Abstract name → descriptive |
| DataGenius | Slalom Legacy Code Translator | Made-up word → clear purpose |
| Experience-Driven Cross-Platform Technology Assessment and Roadmap | Slalom Salesforce Cross-Platform Technology Assessment | Jargon-heavy → simplified |

### Naming Do's
- Lead with Slalom name externally.
- Keep descriptor to 1–2 words.
- Include partner name if developed with or specific to their tech.
- Follow partner brand guidelines (e.g., "Amazon SageMaker" not "AWS Sagemaker").
- Use sub-headers for multi-partner solutions or additional context.
- Be mindful of cultural implications globally.

### Naming Don'ts
- No abstract, jargony, or vague language.
- No symbols, vague acronyms, metaphors, misspellings, or numbers as names.
- No internal-only language or team names.
- No references to other companies' IP/trademarks.
- No language that only Slalomers would understand.

### Naming Checklist
- [ ] Leads with Slalom name
- [ ] Simple and descriptive
- [ ] Tested with people outside your team — they understood it
- [ ] Memorable and to the point
- [ ] Conveys purpose/benefit for target audience
- [ ] No jargon, symbols, or vague acronyms
- [ ] Mindful of global implications
- [ ] No internal-only language
- [ ] Not similar to another company's offering
- [ ] Uses appropriate partner/industry references

## 8. Photography & Imagery

### Principles
- **Tone**: Positive, optimistic, joyful.
- **Scenes**: Authentic, bright, colorful.
- **Color**: Vibrant and saturated, balanced by neutrals. Include brand color pops when possible.
- **Subjects**: Real people and places. High-quality. Low-pretension. Candid, not overly posed.

### Do's
- Genuine and authentic compositions. Candid, unposed, realistic.
- Global-minded — feature wide range of locations, people, cultures.
- Diverse and inclusive — distinct characteristics, lived experiences, multiple identities.
- Capture moments of hope and optimism.
- Realistic depictions of technology and human interaction.

### Don'ts
- No "stock" feeling or inauthentic imagery.
- No dark, dreary, cyberpunk, or dystopian aesthetics.
- No heavily posed or corporate looks.
- No camera effects (light leaks, lens flares, tilt shifts).
- Never use stock to depict Slalom employees.

## 9. Icons & Graphic Elements

### Icons
- Functional yet friendly. Used as accents to simplify complex ideas.
- Preferred: Single-color Slalom Blue or Dark Blue.
- Always pair icons with descriptive text.
- Use 130+ approved icons from the Creative Library.

### Slalom Lines (3 types)
1. **Modular Path** — Curved + straight lines creating unique background shapes.
2. **Bold Single Line** — Swooping lines mimicking logo letterforms.
3. **Macro "S" Crop** — Large geometric shapes from the logo "S", rotated and cropped.

### Object Shapes
- Mix of hard and soft angles (creativity + precision).
- Rectangles for precision. Single rounded corner mimics logo curvature. Fluid waves for flexibility.
- Only ONE rounded corner at a time.

## 10. Accessibility

- Conform to **WCAG 2.1 AA** standards.
- All text, charts, and data graphs must meet contrast requirements.
- Do not rely on color alone to convey meaning — use combination of color, shapes, and text.
- Coral Red and Chartreuse are challenging for color blindness.
- Label all data in charts and apply fill textures/patterns.
